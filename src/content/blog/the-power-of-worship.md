There is a moment in Acts 16 that has always fascinated me. Paul and Silas are in prison. They have been beaten. Their feet are in stocks. It is midnight — the darkest hour, literally and figuratively.

And they are singing.

Not quietly. Loudly enough that the other prisoners can hear them (Acts 16:25). This is not the worship of comfortable people. This is worship as an act of war.

## Worship Is Not a Feeling

We have confused worship with emotion. We say we "felt" worshipful, or we didn't feel anything, as if the value of worship were measured on an emotional scale.

But worship, at its core, is a declaration. It is the act of ascribing worth to God — not because of how you feel, but because of who He is. His worthiness does not fluctuate with your mood.

When you worship in the dry season, you are making a theological statement: *God is still God. His character has not changed. The facts about Him are still true.*

## What Happens When We Worship Against Our Feelings

Paul and Silas sang, and the foundations shook. The doors opened. The chains fell off — not just theirs, but *everyone's* (Acts 16:26).

Authentic worship in difficulty creates atmosphere. Not just for you — for the people around you. Your choice to worship in a hard season may be the very thing that loosens chains in someone else's life.

## Practical Steps

**Start with Scripture, not sentiment.** Open to a Psalm. Read it aloud as prayer. Let God's words form your worship before your emotions have a chance to object.

**Sing older hymns.** The hymn writers often wrote from places of deep suffering. Their words carry a weight and an honesty that can meet you where you are.

**Worship in community.** There will be Sundays when you cannot lift your own voice. The congregation carries you. This is one of the reasons we gather — to hold each other up.
