If you have ever knelt to pray and felt like your words were hitting the ceiling — you are in good company.

David wrote *"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"* (Psalm 22:1). Elijah, after his greatest victory, sat under a tree and asked to die (1 Kings 19:4). Mother Teresa, by her own private confession, lived decades in spiritual dryness while continuing to serve.

Dry seasons are not a sign that God has left. They are a normal — and often necessary — part of the spiritual life.

## Why Dry Seasons Happen

There is no single cause. Sometimes it is sin that has created distance. Sometimes it is exhaustion — spiritual, emotional, or physical. Sometimes God intentionally withdraws the *felt* sense of His presence to deepen our faith, to move us from experience-based faith to Word-based faith.

The worst thing you can do in a dry season is assume the silence means rejection.

## What to Do When You're in One

**Keep showing up.** The discipline of prayer, Scripture, and worship is never more important than when it feels least rewarding. You are training your soul to trust what it cannot feel.

**Be honest with God.** The Psalms give us full permission to pour out grief, confusion, and even complaint before God. He is not fragile. He can handle your honesty.

**Talk to someone.** Isolation deepens dryness. A pastor, a trusted believer, a small group — community is one of God's primary means of grace.

**Look for small graces.** In dry seasons, God often speaks quietly. A verse that catches your eye. A song that unexpectedly moves you. A moment of peace in an anxious week. These are not coincidences.

## The Promise on the Other Side

*"He who goes out weeping, carrying seed to sow, will return with songs of joy, carrying sheaves with him."* — Psalm 126:6

The dry season ends. It always ends. And when it does, you will find that your roots go deeper than they did before.
