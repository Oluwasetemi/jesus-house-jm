> *"I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service."* — Romans 12:1 (KJV)

Paul does not begin with a command. He begins with a plea — *I beseech you* — grounded in the mercies of God that have been unfolded across eleven chapters of Romans. This is not a cold religious duty. It is a response to love.

## What Is a Living Sacrifice?

In the Old Testament, a sacrifice was dead before it reached the altar. The animal had no say in the matter. But Paul calls us to something altogether different: to place ourselves on the altar *alive* — with our will, our choices, our daily decisions — and to stay there.

The challenge of a living sacrifice, as many have noted, is that it keeps crawling off the altar. Every morning is a fresh act of presentation.

## Three Dimensions of This Offering

**1. Your body** — Paul is deliberately physical. This is not merely a spiritual or inward commitment. It includes what you do with your hands, where you take your feet, what you allow your eyes to see. Holiness is embodied.

**2. Holy and acceptable** — The sacrifice must be without blemish. Not perfect in the sense of sinless, but set apart — consecrated for God's use rather than your own.

**3. Your reasonable service** — The Greek word is *logiken*, from which we get *logical*. In light of what God has done for you, this is simply the rational response. Anything less would be unreasonable.

## What This Looks Like in Daily Life

In Kingston, this looks like showing up honestly at work even when it costs you. It looks like treating your neighbour with dignity when the culture says otherwise. It looks like giving God the first hour of your morning before the phone, the news, and the noise.

It is not dramatic. It is daily. And it is the most revolutionary thing a person can do.
